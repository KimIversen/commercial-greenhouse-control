# Deficit Irrigation for Brix/Flavor in Greenhouse Cherry Tomatoes — Research Report

**Date:** 2026-07-01
**Method:** Multi-source deep-research harness — 5 search angles, 22 sources fetched, 94 claims
extracted, top 25 verified by 3-vote adversarial verification (2/3 refutes kills a claim). 21
confirmed, 4 refuted.
**Crop context:** Sun Gold & Black Cherry tomatoes, 50/50 peat + pine-fiber substrate, drip
fertigation, ~2 L/h drippers, weekly Thursday-evening harvest (~18:00), currently time-based pulses
with **no** soil-moisture/tensiometer feedback.

---

## Bottom line

**The intuitive plan — a same-day dryback before the Thursday harvest to sharpen Brix — is not
supported and could backfire.** The evidence points to a different strategy:

1. **Brix is built by a *sustained*, moderate deficit across fruit development — not a terminal
   same-day dryback.** Once fruit reach the pink stage they are hydraulically disconnected from the
   plant's water status; drying the plant at that point no longer raises their sugar.
2. **Cracking is triggered by rehydration *surges* after dry spells.** The anti-split lever for
   split-prone Sun Gold is *steadiness* (steady mild deficit, gentle re-wetting), which means a
   "skip the afternoon, then re-water" cycle is the wrong move.
3. **Cherry/small-fruited types tolerate moderate deficit well on BER** — risk is concentrated in
   large-fruited types and is governed by *early*-stage calcium/water supply.
4. **Moderate beats deep.** Aggressive drybacks lose yield with no extra quality.
5. **The decisive missing input is substrate-moisture feedback.** Every quantitative setpoint in the
   literature is from mineral soil or other substrates, measured with tensiometers / leaf-water
   potential — none of which the current system has. Installing tensiometers unblocks the whole
   strategy.

## Confirmed findings

### 1. A single same-day pre-harvest dryback does NOT meaningfully raise Brix *(high confidence, 3-0)*
Once a fruit reaches the pink stage (30–60% non-green), rising xylem hydraulic resistance
**disconnects it from plant water status** — "once a fruit reached the pink stage of maturity, its
SSC was not affected by subsequent soil moisture stress." Under a *sustained* deficit, by contrast,
late-maturing fruit ran "as much as 1.6 °Brix higher." The Brix window is *before* ripening, applied
continuously.
Sources: J. Exp. Bot. 71(4):1249; Johnstone/Hartz et al., HortScience 40(7):1857; J. Exp. Bot.
(doi:10.1093/jxb/erab001); PMC11592927.

### 2. Sustained moderate deficit (~⅔ of full irrigation) across development raises Brix with little yield loss *(high confidence, 3-0, multiple)*
Convergent primary evidence: a two-thirds-irrigation regime at flowering/fruit development gave "no
significant yield reduction" while improving sugar-to-acid ratio and lowering organic acids; other
trials show increased total soluble solids, sugars, sugar-to-acid ratio, and vitamin C.
Sources: Wang et al. 2019, HortScience 54(8):1409; Agric. Water Manag. 161:9–19; PMC11592927;
Postharvest Biol. Technol. 2022 (S0925521422002800).

### 3. Closest-match study: greenhouse cherry 'Summerbrix' under RDI — higher TSS/sugars, ~10% (non-significant) yield loss, 53% water saving *(high confidence, 3-0)*
Corell et al., *Agriculture* 2021, 11(7):669. RDI fruit had higher TSS "mainly due to a greater
accumulation of both fructose and glucose and lower content of citric acid," with cumulative yield
"not significantly different." **Caveat:** measured flavor *volatiles* showed "no significant and
clear effect" — the Brix/sugar-acid balance improved, but a clean sensory-flavor win was not proven.
Cultivar is Summerbrix (not Sun Gold/Black Cherry); soil was clay-loam (not peat/pine); scheduling
was by leaf water potential.

### 4. Safe deficit depth is bounded — moderate wins, deep loses *(high confidence)*
Keep stress mild: midday leaf water potential more positive than ~ -0.9 MPa (the 2024 follow-up
tightened this to -0.7/-0.8 MPa). Lowering media potential -30 → -60 hPa raised TSS ~5% and dry
matter ~12.5%; but an aggressive dryback to -50 kPa cut yield 15.9–26.1% vs a -20 kPa optimum.
**Absolute setpoints do not transfer to 50/50 peat+pine — soilless media dry back far faster than
soil.** Use for *direction and relative severity*, not literal numbers.
Sources: MDPI 11/7/669; PMC4688453; PLOS ONE pone.0142204; PMC10781057.

### 5. Steady controlled deficit REDUCES cracking in small-fruited types *(medium confidence, 3-0, single study)*
In small-fruited 'Kabiria', increasing water stress across -30 to -90 hPa *linearly lowered*
unmarketable loss, "mainly due to a reduced occurrence of fruit cracking." Cracking is classically
triggered by a **rehydration surge after a dry period**, so the actionable lesson is a *steady*
moderate deficit with no abrupt rewetting. **Caveat:** 'Kabiria' is a 40–47 mm cocktail type (larger
than true ~15–20 mm Sun Gold); method was steady subirrigation, not a drip dryback-then-rewet — so
extrapolation to Sun Gold is by size-class analogy.
Source: Signore et al. 2015, Front. Plant Sci. (PMC4688453).

### 6. BER risk from deficit is low in cherry types; protect EARLY-stage water/calcium *(high confidence)*
Large-fruited 'Diana' nearly doubled unmarketable yield (BER) under stress; small-fruited 'Kabiria'
"showed higher adaptation to water stress." Because late-stage fruit is hydraulically
resistant, "transport of calcium in the early stage will largely determine fruit calcium
accumulation." So a BER-safe regime protects *young* fruit's water supply; moderate deficit itself is
not a strong BER trigger for cherry types. (A hypothesis that drought directly causes BER via ROS was
**refuted, 0-3**.)
Sources: PMC4688453; J. Exp. Bot. 71(4):1249.

### 7. Deficit effects are cultivar/timing/intensity dependent — specify stage + moderate severity *(high confidence)*
"Studies… have shown positive, null, and negative results." Field processing-tomato ranges
(20–60% ETo over ripening; cutoff 40–50 d preharvest loses yield) confirm the *direction* (sustained
partial deficit beats an abrupt cutoff) but **do not transfer as setpoints** — a 20% ETo target that
is survivable in high-buffer soil would collapse a small soilless root zone in 1–2 days.
Sources: J. Exp. Bot. 71(4):1249; ResearchGate 269396023; Agric. Water Manag. 133 (S0378377413003235).

### Practitioner crop-steering (blogs/trade, lower evidence tier)
- **Generative steering** (toward fruit quality) = *fewer, larger* shots with *bigger* overnight
  drybacks; **vegetative/steady** = *more frequent, smaller* shots, shallow dryback.
- Reported generative dryback magnitudes vary widely by source (4–6%, 8–10%, even 15–30% substrate
  moisture drop) — treat as ranges to explore against your own tensiometer data, not gospel.
- End irrigation before sunset for a gentle overnight dryback.
Sources: AROYA, Growlink, Greenhouse Grower, Hortidaily crop-steering guides.

## Refuted claims (killed in verification)
- "Deficit during flowering/development matters more than at maturation" — **1-2**, mixed.
- "60% field capacity / irrigate every 6 days gives +37% Brix" as a transferable setpoint — **1-2**
  (the +37% figure is real for that study's substrate but not a general target).
- "BER rises once salinity hits 3–4 dS/m" as a general safety threshold — **1-2**.
- "Drought directly causes BER via ROS (not Ca)" — **0-3**.

---

## Key caveats (why we measure before we steer aggressively)
- **Cultivar gap:** only one source is greenhouse cherry; none tested Sun Gold or Black Cherry.
- **Substrate gap:** setpoints come from mineral soil / other mixes; 50/50 peat+pine dries back much
  faster and has far lower buffering — "gentle in soil" can be "a crash in substrate."
- **Measurement gap:** the strong safety thresholds need tensiometer / leaf-water-potential data.
  This is exactly what installing tensiometers fixes.
- **Crop-type gap:** processing-tomato studies have one terminal harvest; an indeterminate cherry
  under weekly picking carries green + ripening + ripe fruit + flowers at once, so "withhold through
  ripening" is not literally executable — the deficit must be a *continuous background* level.
- **Single-dryback ineffectiveness is inferential:** no study ran a same-day dryback head-to-head vs
  sustained deficit; the conclusion rests on pink-stage hydraulic physiology plus within-study
  superiority of sustained regimes.

## Open questions the tensiometers will help answer
1. What is the actual dryback curve of *this* 50/50 peat+pine substrate, and what pulse count/volume
   holds it in a mild-deficit band without collapsing?
2. For Sun Gold specifically, does steady mild deficit reduce cracking, and what overnight dryback
   depth is safe before the morning re-wet triggers splitting?
3. What last-irrigation-before-sunset timing + morning re-wet ramp minimizes the rehydration surge
   while keeping the Brix benefit?
4. Does deficit genuinely improve *sensory* flavor in Sun Gold/Black Cherry, or only Brix/sugar-acid?
